How to read the chart in a minute

Three lines. The grey dashed one carries the trend: a power law with saturation, cycles removed. It works as the backbone to which the asset returns, and by itself predicts nothing. The blue one multiplies that backbone by the cycle overlay. The orange one shows what happened.

The lower panel carries the residual, measured in dex. Dex expresses “how many times” in logarithmic form: +1 dex puts price ten times above the trend, −0.3 dex lowers it roughly by half, zero falls exactly on the backbone.

As of August 2026 the residual holds around −0.36 dex, that is, price is a little below half the trend. It looks worse than it behaves: every trough in the history of bitcoin parked between −0.24 and −0.38 dex. A trough is supposed to look like this. The alarming signal would be a residual that leaves the ±0.5 dex band and stays there: that is falsification criterion 5, which has never fired for longer than five months.

The peak and the trough in the table are nodes of the model, that is, the trend of the corresponding month multiplied by the calibrated amplitude. The series of observations on the chart runs on monthly closes, the nodes on intra-month extremes, which gives small discrepancies between the table and the orange line.

What the model is assembled from

Three independent layers, multiplied by one another.

Layer 1. The power law — why price grows at all

At first, intuition about bitcoin often said “exponential growth”, but that would be a strong oversimplification. An exponential doubles over equal intervals: a year, another year, another year. A power law needs an ever longer stretch for the same multiple each time.

The formula P ∝ t3β reads as follows: price grows as the age of the network raised to the power 3β, the age counted in days from the genesis block of 3 January 2009. At the measured β = 1.897 the exponent equals 5.69, and it was never fitted to the chart. It is given by two separately measured quantities multiplied together, each with a physical reading:

3The number of addresses in the network grew roughly as the cube of its age. An observed fact, not an assumption.
1.897The value of the network grows faster than the number of participants — Metcalfe's law in generalised form. Every new participant adds not only himself but also connections to everyone else. The value 1.897 is obtained empirically and lies between textbook Metcalfe (n²) and Odlyzko (n log n).
= 5.69Their product and the source of the exponent.

Hence the logarithmic scale on the chart, where each division means a factor of ten. A power law straightens out on such a scale, and deviations from it become visible to the eye. Proportions are preserved as well: a fall from $100 to $50 looks like a fall from $100,000 to $50,000, because for the owner both mean a half.

Two free parameters and a way to check them

Layer 1 carries two fitted numbers: the exponent and the multiplier. These two have to reproduce 194 monthly observations spanning six orders of magnitude in price, and the residual leaves ±0.5 dex in only 14 of them — almost exclusively on the blow-offs of 2011 and 2013/14, where price itself behaved abnormally.

Exactly those 14 out of 194 are what the miss counter below the β slider displays, so the claim “β is measured, not chosen” is not taken on faith. Move the β slider to 1.70 — the misses become 26, and more than half of the new ones will fall on 2010–2012: early history falls off first, because it is exactly what sets the slope. A near doubling from a shift of one tenth is the content of the word “measured”.

When β changes, the curve is recalibrated through a pivot point at the logarithmic centre of the observations — day 2971 from genesis, February 2017. The rotation happens around the centre of mass of the data rather than around an arbitrary end, otherwise the slider would break the fit for a reason having nothing to do with the exponent itself.

Whether all of this counts as evidence or as curve fitting is taken apart below, in the objections, since it comes up first thing every time.

Layer 2. Saturation — why this cannot go on forever

Cubic growth of the network runs into the obvious: there is a finite number of people on the planet. Any network — telephone, internet, payment — travels the same path: first acceleration, then slowdown, then a plateau. An S-shaped curve.

In the model this is arranged as soft braking: P = Ptrend · L / (Ptrend³ + L³)⅓, where L is the level of the plateau. The mechanics are simple: far below the plateau the braking is almost imperceptible, closer to the plateau it drags. The cubic exponent governs how soft the transition feels, stretching it over years instead of a step.

How the ceiling enters the plateau

The level of the plateau follows from the audience ceiling through L ∝ Wβ (the same Metcalfe exponent): plateau = 657,000 · (W / 1610)β. The exponent β is close to two, so the ceiling enters the plateau roughly as a square — hence the sharpness the slider warns about.

It is this link that the “owner ceiling” slider exposes. Move it around: the whole right-hand part of the chart will travel up and down, while the mechanics underneath remain untouched. The travel of the slider measures what share of the 2030s rests on a quantity nobody has counted.

Why the ceiling is not counted in addresses

Today (August 2026) roughly 106 M owners map onto 57 M non-zero addresses, and the ratio is falling: custodial services and exchange-traded funds keep taking in holders who never touch an address on the blockchain. Measuring the size of the network in addresses makes less sense with every year that passes.

As long as the decay reads as moderate, the contradiction is tolerable. If, however, the decay is irreversible, the contradiction becomes direct: one cannot simultaneously claim that euphoria died because of a change in the composition of holders and count the ceiling as though the ratio of addresses to owners had stayed historical. That is why the plateau is computed from the number of owners. The number of addresses does not enter the calculation at all.

Layer 3. Cycles — why the road is not straight

Historically, every halving was followed by the same sequence: acceleration, a euphoric peak roughly a year and a half later, a crash, a long trough.

The explanation through the reduction of issuance, however, converges poorly: the market knows the date of the halving years in advance, and a fully anticipated change in issuance ought to be priced in continuously, without a delayed impulse in the eighteenth month. The model here rests on a sentiment-phase explanation:

Sustained growth begins when the majority of new participants finally stop believing in growth. Sustained decline begins when the majority absorb the belief that growth is infinite.

The logic is that at the trough the beliefs of participants are maximally heterogeneous: everyone has his own reasons for scepticism, there is no single narrative. Such a state is unstable and prone to reassembly around a new idea. At the peak everything is the opposite: everyone believes the same thing, and precisely for that reason everyone's exit thresholds coincide, so that a single push triggers simultaneous selling. Unanimity looks like robustness and works as fragility.

Technically the cycle is defined by three nodes: the peak 18 months after the halving, the trough at 26–30, a return to the trend by the next halving. Between the nodes runs a smooth cosine interpolation.

The timing of the nodes is taken from history. Peaks arrived in months 13, 17, 18 and 18 after the four halvings, that is, the lag lengthened and then settled, and 18 is carried forward. Maximum declines fell in months 26, 29, 30 and 26. The timing turned out to be far more stable than the amplitude, and the latter currently looks like it is collapsing.

How the swing is distributed between top and bottom

The proportion between the top and the bottom flipped over across those same four cycles. In the early ones almost the whole swing went upwards: in H1 the peak rose 1.03 dex above the trend while the trough sank only 0.28 — four fifths of the swing fell on euphoria. Afterwards the share of the top fell monotonically: 77% in H2, 55% in H3 and only 14% in H4, where the peak barely detached from the trend while the trough went to the usual −0.37.

Here a second indicator of maturation emerges, independent of the general compression of amplitude: the market loses the capacity for euphoria noticeably faster than the capacity for panic.

It follows that the top and the bottom are two different sequences rather than one with a fixed proportion. Peaks: 1.03, 0.81, 0.46, 0.06, a monotonic decline. Troughs: −0.28, −0.24, −0.38, −0.37, no trend at all. The model therefore projects them independently, with two sliders, and does not force the decay of one phase to drag the other along.

Consistency of the decays

Asymmetric decay — the top fades, the bottom stands — is the signature of institutionalisation: the composition of holders changed, euphoric buyers were replaced by funds and custodians, panic remained with those who still hold directly.

Symmetric decay — both phases fade — is the signature of an epistemic shift. The sentiment-phase mechanism requires a maximum of belief heterogeneity at the trough; such a state is impossible to assemble if participants rely on a shared trustworthy source instead of their own scattered reasons for scepticism. This is the only known process that must damp panic together with euphoria.

So far only asymmetry is observed. Symmetry, however, can only be tested in September 2030.

What the residual panel shows

The lower panel removes the growth itself and leaves only the deviation from the trend. The humps compress in sequence: 1.03 → 0.81 → 0.46 → 0.06 dex.

The December 2013 peak stood ten times above the trend. The December 2017 peak — six times. The November 2021 peak — three times. The October 2025 peak exceeded the trend by 15% — this is the first cycle in the history of the asset in which a euphoric phase effectively did not happen.

Hence the fork around which this part of the model is built. Either the decay is irreversible — and then bitcoin leaves the speculative category and settles into an ordinary mature network asset with moderate fluctuations. Or the collapse was temporary — and then the height of the peaks will return as soon as the environment around the market changes. The first slider at the top switches precisely between these two hypotheses: zero means that the decay of euphoria arrived “permanently”, one means the decay of euphoria is “temporary”. The bottom lives by its own rules, and the second slider is set aside for it.

Where to set the sliders

There are four sliders in all, and they are not equals. The two decays rule the next five years. The ceiling affects the height of the H5 peak moderately — the plausible range moves it from $331k to $387k — while it single-handedly runs the 2030s and decides how much of the cycle is left by the later epochs.

The decay sliders, moreover, are not independent of each other. Their compatibility is brought out by a separate indicator above the chart, because one combination is internally contradictory while looking perfectly normal by the numbers.

The indicator must be read carefully. It checks for the presence of a mechanism, not the soundness of a setting. The contradictory combination is: euphoria fades irreversibly, panic does not fade at all. The indicator marks such a combination red not because the observation is wrong, but because there is no explanation for it: the cause that killed the top is obliged sooner or later to reach the bottom.

The converse is true as well. A compatible setting does not mean a well-founded one. “Cycle never broke” passes the check without remarks, but it requires treating H4 as a fluke, and there is (as yet) no independent evidence of that. That is why the indicator is a neutral colour rather than green: it catches one specific inconsistency rather than passing judgement.

Owner ceiling: 1300–1900 M

The scale is read through market capitalisation and gold, as a distantly similar non-digital analogue. By the mid-2030s about 20.7 M coins will be in circulation, while all the gold ever mined is worth roughly $31 trillion today. At 6 billion adults the ruler looks like this:

The familiar anchor is the share of adults owning equities, 15–25%. But this share is low for two reasons: most people have no investable surplus, and there is a barrier to entry — a broker, literacy, trust in the procedure. The second reason is removed over a decade by an advisor available to everyone (AI), the first expands with growth itself. An upper bound of 25% with the friction removed looks more like a lower one, hence 22–32% and a range of 1300–1900 M owners.

These figures are recalculated live under the chart, in the “What this asks of money” block. There the same capitalisation is shown not only at the plateau but along the way to it: how much has to be added per year on average, and how much over the next twelve months. It also makes visible that issuance is under one percent of the required growth and falling: by 2028 the halving stops meaning anything quantitatively as a supply mechanism, and the cycle is left entirely to demand.

The base stands at 1610 M — this is exactly the same calculation the previous version gave at 866 M addresses: the same $657k plateau, the same $13.6T capitalisation, the same 44% of the value of all the gold ever mined. Redenominating into owners simply changed the units. The argument for a higher ceiling remains an argument and lives further to the right along the scale, not at the base of the calculation.

Euphoria decay: 0.00

Three reference positions:

0.00the break in H4 continues: the H5 peak rises 0.03 dex above the trend, and beyond that the peaks fade away almost entirely along with the inflow
0.50the decay has slowed: the H5 peak around 0.11 dex, twice as high as H4

Four cycles of evidence. The fact of the decline rests on all four observations at once and holds firmly. The sharpness of the last step rests on one observation — on H4, and nothing more is known about it.

Hence the position of the slider. There is nowhere further left to go: zero simply continues what has already happened. Moving right is possible, but it has to be paid for with an assumption — H4 must be declared a fluke. There is no evidence for that, and nowhere to get it: price alone cannot yield it, an outside source is needed. Further down the page this assumption is called the H4 caveat.

A possible test of the explanation

The irreversibility of the decay rests not on the fact of it but on a specific mechanism: institutionalisation. Euphoric buyers were replaced by funds and custodians, and the market's capacity to overheat disappeared along with them.

This mechanism has its own observable trace, in no way connected to price — the ratio of the number of non-zero addresses to the number of owners. A holder through a fund never touches an address, so the further the replacement has gone, the lower the ratio. It has been falling for years and now stands at about 0.54: roughly 57 M addresses per 106 M owners.

Hence a test that can be run monthly and without any model at all. If the ratio stops falling while the peaks stay dead, institutionalisation will stop explaining the decay — and beneath the zero position of the slider there will be no mechanism left, only four observations of a trend. A continuing fall works the other way: it is an argument for irreversibility drawn from data that has nothing to do with price.

A caveat is obligatory. The ratio is a trace of this mechanism, not of decay in general. The market could have matured for other reasons — deeper derivatives, less leverage — and then the ratio has every right to stand still while the decay persists. A flat ratio refutes the explanation, not the slider itself.

Panic decay: there is no data

There is not a single piece of evidence here. Four troughs over fifteen years stand at the same depth, no movement is visible in them, and the direct reading says to set zero: the bottom did not fade before and will not fade later.

One consideration works against zero, and it comes from how the mechanism is built. Zero on the upper slider asserts that euphoria disappeared for a reason rather than by chance. The reason may be any of several — the composition of holders changed, custody moved to institutions, participants gained access to a common trustworthy source of information — but each of them is by mechanism obliged sooner or later to reach the bottom.

The lag, meanwhile, is explicable. The top of the cycle is made by arriving participants, the bottom by those who stay, and the composition of the latter changes far more slowly. Panic decay should therefore run behind euphoria decay, but run it should.

This makes the combination “zero above, zero below” internally contradictory. It means that the cause which killed euphoria acts on exactly half of the cycle and never touches the bottom, and there is nothing to explain such selectivity with.

Observable marker, September 2030

The H5 trough falls in September 2030. The four previous troughs stood in the corridor −0.24…−0.38, but the observation can no longer be compared with that corridor directly: by 2029 saturation lifts the trough by itself, and with panic undimmed the model expects −0.31 dex. Evidence that panic has faded would be a trough shallower than −0.16 dex — what the right edge of the slider gives at the base ceiling. The gap between −0.31 and −0.16 reads as partial fading and is handled by the slider.

A trough in the familiar corridor means the opposite: only euphoria was fading, the mechanism of the cycle remained human, the panic slider must be set to zero and one must live with the red indicator as an honestly recorded anomaly.

β: not for tuning

The only substantive objection to the value 1.897 is that institutional custody thins the graph of connections between participants and ought to shift the scaling towards n log n. The objection is correct by mechanism and refuted by the data: move the slider and look at the miss counter. The range within which the fit holds is very narrow, and that is the answer.

Why the base is the “Quiet cycle”

Of the four scenarios the second button from the left is chosen as the base, and the page opens on it by default. Below are the choice itself and its price; the justifications are worked through above, each in its own section.

Euphoria — zero. The setting is derived entirely from the data. There is nowhere further left to move, and further right only by accepting the H4 caveat.

Panic — a half. There is no data beneath this setting; it stands on the requirement of coherence, that the cause which put out the top is obliged in time to reach the bottom. A half means the process is granted to be under way, while the question of how far it has gone is left open.

Ceiling — 1610 M. Follows from the same premise as the two settings above: if institutionalisation has gone far enough to kill euphoria, it has also removed the barrier to entry and moved a sizeable part of holding to custodians.

The price of the choice. The panic slider is not supported by a single observation. It stands at 0.50 out of considerations of coherence, and the first real evidence will appear only in September 2030. Until then half of the base scenario rests on reasoning.

Why the scenarios stand in this order

The four scenarios are four points on a single axis. The axis answers one question: how irreversibly bitcoin has stopped being a speculative asset. Maturity on the left, speculation on the right.

Three of the four sliders move along this axis in concert, and beneath every coupling stands one and the same mechanism. Institutional custody damps euphoria because it changes the composition of buyers. It also raises the owner ceiling because it removes the barrier to entry: the lion's share of people do not own an asset of this class because of the broker, literacy and trust in the procedure, rather than for lack of interest. One process — both consequences. β stays at its measured value in all four scenarios; it is not meant for tuning.

The direction here is easy to read backwards, so let me put it plainly. The owner ceiling works on this axis as a fuel reserve. It counts how many people have yet to arrive, and on the left edge there are the most of them. What damps the cycle is the upper slider: it sets what share of the arrivals is capable of producing euphoria. On the left there is plenty of fuel and it barely ignites. On the right there is less fuel, and it burns entirely.

Because of this the cycle has four times more fuel left on the left edge of the axis than on the right. It is dead there for a different reason: arriving participants have stopped producing euphoria. This is the one place on the page where two indicators point in opposite directions, and both point correctly.

Hence monotonicity down both columns at once:

Plateau$812k → $657k → $470k → $438k. The more mature the asset, the wider the terminal ownership and the higher the level of saturation. The step narrows on the right: the column runs into the lower edge of the plausible corridor of 1300–1900 M.
Drawdown21% → 33% → 51% → 70%. An obvious increase in speculativeness, and the gap between the edges is now wider — saturation takes more away from the scenarios on the left than from those on the right.

The column of peaks does not obey monotonicity, and the reason is what was just said. The H5 peak runs $384k → $373k → $408k → $510k, that is, it is higher in the scenarios that are more optimistic by mechanism and more pessimistic in substance. The peak here is the product of two factors moving towards each other: the fuel remaining falls to the right, the share that ignites grows to the right, and on the 2029 horizon the second factor outweighs, because saturation has barely engaged by that date. This should be read as follows: a high peak in 2029 points to a live cycle, which will be followed by the customary crash. In the “Cycle never broke” scenario the peak is a third higher and the trough twice as low.

Moving right along the axis changes not only the height of the cycle but what it rests on. On the left the 2030s cycle follows the inflow: the inflow fades, the cycle fades, and the claim is checked by the address count of criterion 6. On the right a tenth of the inflow survives to 2033 while the cycle keeps its full amplitude, and the difference is carried by the euphoria slider — that is, by the premise that the speculative character of the asset survived on its own. There is not a single observation beneath that premise. The diagnostic below the ceiling slider shows this proportion for epoch H6.

And hence, too, the way to use the buttons. They are not “optimistic — base — pessimistic”: by the price in 2035 the left edge is optimistic, by the price in October 2029 the right edge is. The choice between them is a choice of hypothesis about what happened to the market, not a choice of a desired number.

Objections I expect, and what I concede

A power law fits anything on log-log axes.

Partially answered

Fair as a general warning, and for that reason the exponent was never fitted to price. It is derived from two separately measured quantities: the number of addresses against time (3) and value against the number of addresses (1.897). Layer 1 carries two free numbers in total, set against 194 monthly points across six orders of magnitude. The useful check looks at the structure of the residual; goodness of fit decides little here. The residual does have structure: humps tied to the halvings, decreasing monotonically. Fitting artefacts do not produce those.

The question would be closed by falsification criterion 5: the residual leaves ±0.5 dex and does not return for two years. The criterion is set for use — if it fires the model is thrown out, and recalibration would be cheating.

You are modelling the single asset that happened to survive.

Conceded, there is no answer inside the model

The strongest objection on the list, and I have nothing to set against it. Every network that died also had an early growth phase resembling a power law, and curves are fitted only to the survivor. The model is conditional by construction: it describes bitcoin under the persistence of the current regime and assigns no probability to that regime ending. Read as an unconditional expectation, it misleads. Read as “if the adoption mechanism holds, the shape looks like this”, it works, with the probability of the premise supplied from outside.

The four-year cycle is dead, everyone knows this already.

Half agreed, and with a number

The model agrees more than it argues, and puts a number under the agreement: H4 rose 0.06 dex above the trend against 1.03 for H1. The euphoric top died. The other two parts of the cycle outlived it. The timing held — 18 months to the peak, the same as H3. And the bottom did not fade at all: the troughs of the four cycles stand at −0.28, −0.24, −0.38, −0.37 dex, with no trend whatsoever. The formula “the cycle is dead” lumps three different things into one. One of them is gone, the other two remain.

You calibrated the H4 trough on June 2026 and then declared that the model converges.

Conceded, flagged on the spot

Yes. The lower node of H4 is taken from the observed minimum of $57,950, so the agreement there is tautological and checks nothing. The table of cycles states this directly on the spot, without burying it in notes. Falsification criterion 1 exists for the same reason: if price settles below $58,000, the node was not a trough, and the amplitude calibration on which everything further depends falls apart. Put plainly: the amplitude of H4 is a measurement, and all the predictive content of the model begins with H5.

Addresses are not users. Your ceiling measures the wrong thing.

Conceded — the weakest link

The objection was correct for the first version of the model (see below), and it took the form of a rebuild. The plateau is no longer counted in addresses: it is taken directly from the number of owners, 657,000 · (W / 1610)β, and the number of addresses has been removed from the calculation altogether.

The inconvenient part survived the rebuild: the very same institutionalisation by which the decay of the cycles is explained simultaneously breaks the measuring instrument. One process produces both effects. The difference is that the model no longer leans on a shrinking instrument: the decay of the cycles and the level of the plateau have stopped depending on one and the same breaking quantity.

Still open: the estimate of the number of owners is itself poorly measured. 106 M is a digest of industry estimates, not a census.

Where does Metcalfe's 1.897 come from? Looks fitted after the fact.

Answered, with a caveat about sensitivity

The value comes from Santostasi & Perrenod (2026), where it is derived from tests of scale invariance and not chosen for the beauty of the chart. It is neither the textbook Metcalfe n² nor Odlyzko's n log n. Entering the calculation twice makes β a sensitive parameter, and in the first version this sensitivity was nowhere displayed. Now it is: β is brought out into a slider, and the miss counter below it counts the months outside the ±0.5 dex band. The check works both ways. It confirms that 1.897 is computed sensibly, and it also shows how narrow the admissible corridor is.

There are no confidence intervals here. This is a line, not a forecast.

Partially conceded

The blue band sweeps the ceiling ±40%, carries no statistical content, and the page labels it exactly that way. The historical monthly scatter around the central path is about 0.30 dex (1σ), so an honest 1σ envelope for any individual month would be roughly four times as wide and by the 2030s would completely swallow the ceiling band. Showing the scatter from a parameter rather than from variance was a deliberate choice: with an estimate of the ceiling the reader can argue, with variance he can do almost nothing.

$380k in 2029 is absurdly little / absurdly much.

Depends on which half you dispute

These two claims are worth separating, because the model answers them differently. The 2029 peak falls in the confident half: from $373k to $510k across all four scenarios, a spread of 1.4×, since saturation has barely engaged on such a term. That whole spread comes from the euphoria slider; the owner ceiling affects the height of the H5 peak only moderately, deciding something else instead — how much of the cycle is left by the later epochs. To dispute the figure means to dispute the power law itself. The 2030s make up the fragile half: the same four scenarios give a plateau from $438k to $812k, a wider spread, and the ceiling can reasonably be disputed beyond that range in either direction. Disagreement about 2035 is cheap and expected. Disagreement about 2029 requires an argument about the exponent.

The sentiment-phase story is unfalsifiable hand-waving.

Partially conceded

The mechanism justifies the shape of the cycle, but the shape itself is calibrated on observed timings and amplitudes and would look exactly the same had the original explanation been purely mechanical. The narrative layer thus does interpretive work; take it out and the model will compute exactly the same numbers. What the story buys is a ground for expecting decay at all: as the heterogeneity of beliefs at the trough declines together with the growth of institutional participation, reassembly around a single narrative weakens. This is a directional prediction specifically about euphoria, and euphoria is what collapsed. No more than a suggestive consideration, carried down into the limits below.

What could falsify this

Six conditions, from the nearest to the most distant:

1Price settles below $58,000. Then late June 2026 was not the trough of the cycle, and with it the lower node of H4 falls apart — the very node by which the amplitude of the decay is set. Testable in the coming months. now $64k
2The drawdown of the current cycle deeper than 70% from the October 2025 peak, that is, a move below ≈$37,900. The shallowest collapse of previous cycles is 78% in H3, so at 70% H4 stops differing from them qualitatively and the whole extrapolation of the decay loses its footing: it is held up in essence by a single observation. now 54%
3The H5 trough stops shallower than −0.16 dex. The one item on the list the model survives being triggered: panic has faded in the wake of euphoria, the panic slider goes to the right, the mechanism of the cycle has ceased to be purely human. The threshold has moved from −0.24: part of the shallowing of the trough is now explained by saturation through the multiplier ζ, and −0.16 dex is what the right edge of the panic slider gives at the base ceiling. September 2030.
4The drawdown of cycle H5 deeper than 60% at zero euphoria decay. Rejects irreversible decay in favour of a temporary collapse. At the base ceiling, euphoria decay 0 and panic decay 1 the model gives 20%; at the reverse arrangement — 68%.
5The residual log₁₀(observed / trend) leaves ±0.5 dex and does not return for more than two years in a row. It would mean that the power-law regime is broken and the model cannot be applied at all. 6The number of non-zero addresses grows more slowly than 3/t for more than two years in a row. The exponent 5.69 = 3 × 1.897 rests on the cubic growth of the network, so this is a check on the slope rather than on cycle amplitude: if the cube has broken, the whole trajectory is too steep, whatever any slider is set to. At a network age of 17.6 years the model requires 17.0% per year — that is, from today's 57 M to 68 M in a year and 80 M in two. Saturation does not yet soften that bar: at the base ceiling it takes off only a tenth of a percent. Where the threshold in the second condition comes from

The threshold is set by the history of drawdowns, without any love for round numbers. H1, H2 and H3 fell by 85, 84 and 78 per cent; H4 stands today at 54. Twenty-four points separate it from the shallowest of the earlier cycles, and it is exactly this margin that fills the claim “H4 is built differently” with content. A threshold of 70% leaves a small gap: below it a formal difference still remains, but there is no longer anything with which to explain it.

What the model does not do

It does not predict the price of a particular month. The scatter around the central line within a year is comparable to the line itself, and an attempt to read it as “in March it will be such-and-such” is an abuse.

It does not account for discontinuities: regulatory bans in major jurisdictions, cryptographic threats, macro shocks. All three layers describe the behaviour of a system remaining in the current regime.

It does not distinguish cause from effect in the sentiment-phase mechanism. The observed link between the heterogeneity of beliefs and reversals is real, but it remains an empirical regularity with no proven mechanism beneath it.

It does not model the supply side at all — lost coins, the behaviour of long-term holders, exchange balances, mining economics. Everything connected with supply is implicitly folded into the two fitted constants of the first layer.

And the main thing it does not do

The plateau is tied to a structure of world wealth recognisably similar to today's: the scale of the ceiling is read through a share of the value of all the gold ever mined. The cycles assume a market of human participants with heterogeneous beliefs, operating up to 2040. Both premises are a bet that no transformative technological discontinuities occur within the horizon of the model.

The premise is nowhere set as a parameter, but it is stronger than the exponent β and stronger than the estimate of the ceiling: an error in those moves the numbers, while an error in this one cancels applicability itself. The panic decay slider is the only place on this page where it becomes observable, because a shared trustworthy source of information for all participants at once — a strong publicly available AI, for instance — is exactly the discontinuity that would give itself away through a decay of the bottom.

What changed relative to the first version

The original model was published in April 2026 and gave a single number: a plateau of about $565,000 by the mid-2030s. Its calibration started from a spot price of $74,500, already out of date by August. Here the model has been rebuilt in three places.

The rebuild aimed at the form of the result. The central path barely moved: the geometric centre of the ceiling range gives a plateau of $657k against the original $565k, a difference of 16%. In place of a point forecast there is now a claim with its uncertainty marked out and with an indication of exactly which measurement would narrow that uncertainty.

Two conceptual problems were fixed along the way. The first: addresses served as a measure of the size of the network, whereas the link between addresses and living people is breaking down — 106 M owners correspond to about 57 M non-zero addresses, and the ratio is falling as custodial services and exchange-traded funds grow. Now the plateau is computed from owners, and addresses have been removed from the calculation.

The second: the cycle was described by a single number with a fixed distribution between top and bottom. In the data these are two different sequences — the peaks fall monotonically, the troughs stand level — and reducing them to one parameter erased the only substantive signal. Now they are separated into two sliders, and a dedicated indicator checks whether their settings are compatible.

How to reproduce this

The model takes about forty lines of JavaScript in the source of the page, with no dependencies apart from Chart.js for drawing. The page is static: everything is computed in the browser, not a single request goes outside. To check or extend it:

  • pure(t, β) — the power law with recalibration through a pivot point at day 2971 from genesis
  • sat(p, L) — the saturation envelope, p·L / (p³ + L³)^⅓
  • plateau(W, β) — 657000 · (W/1610)^β, the level of the plateau from the number of owners
  • amps(de, dp, W, β) — the separate projection of peak and trough amplitudes onto H5–H8; euphoria is interpolated between 0.030 and 0.207 dex, panic is divided by 1 − 0.5·dp each cycle, after which both are multiplied by the ζ of the corresponding epoch
  • tieState(de, dp) — the compatibility state of the two decays
  • phiAt(t, W, β) — the share of inflow surviving saturation; zetaAt — the share of the cycle that remains: d_e + (1 − d_e)·φ
  • netG(t, W, β) — the rate of inflow implied by the model: (1/β)·dln(trend)/dt
  • fuelState(W, de, β) — what the 2030s cycle rests on
  • EPOCHS — the table of halvings with per-epoch amplitudes a (peak, dex above trend) and b (trough, dex below trend)
  • modAt(i, A) — the cycle overlay, cosine interpolation between the halving, the peak, the trough and the next halving
  • OBS — the observed monthly series since July 2010

The OBS array contains approximate monthly closes compiled from public sources. For the shape of the residual this is enough; for anything requiring precision it is not — before any serious use replace it with a verified series from a single consistent source.
